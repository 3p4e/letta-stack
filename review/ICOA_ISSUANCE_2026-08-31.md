## Why 81 iCoAs and not 102

Identity and foreign matter are properties of the material, determined once. A CoQ reissued
because the **cannabinoids and mycotoxins** were re-analysed (the Farmahem 197-series) covers
the same batch: the same macroscopy, the same microscopy, the same TLC, the same foreign
matter. So the reissued CoQ **references the iCoA already issued** rather than triggering a
second one.

That is why the parameter counts above are given per CoQ *and* per batch — 90 CoQ documents
will carry an Ident A statement, but only 69 iCoAs have to be written to supply it.

The two groups are also disjoint, which simplifies the work: **no batch on the Ph. Eur. form
needs a reissue.** The 12 Ident-C-only batches are all from June–July 2026 and none carries a
197-series analysis.

## Where the split comes from, and why it can be trusted

CNP changed its certificate form in mid-2026:

| | Older DAB form | **Ph. Eur. 11.5 form** |
|---|---|---|
| Certificates | 61 | **12** |
| Identification — macroscopy (A) | not reported | `Одговара` |
| Identification — microscopy (B) | not reported | `Одговара` |
| Identification — TLC (C) | not reported | **not reported** |
| Foreign matter | not reported | `макс. 2.00 %` |
| Loss on drying, cannabinoids | reported | reported |

A batch's iCoA scope therefore depends entirely on which form its CNP certificate uses — and
**all 73 CNP certificates were read off their own pages on 31.08.2026**, so this is not
inferred from a filename, a parse or a retrieval index.

The gap analysis independently marks exactly twelve batches `IdentC only`. The twelve it names
and the twelve carrying a Ph. Eur. certificate **agree with no difference in either
direction** — the page reads confirm the model rather than replacing it.

`ППК26110` · `26111` · `26112` · `26113` · `26114` · `26115` (30.06.2026) ·
`26116` · `26117` · `26118` · `26119` (06.07.2026) · `26127` · `26128` (21.07.2026)

**Identification C has never been performed by any laboratory.** Not on the older form, and
not on the Ph. Eur. form either — the twelve pages print `Идентификација: Макроскопија,
Микроскопија` and stop. It is discharged in-house by risk analysis with scientific
justification, using the qualitative determination from the HPLC cannabinoid assay. That is
why Ident C appears on all 102 CoQ documents and every one of the 81 iCoAs.

## Four things to settle before working through the register

### 1 · `FB032601` (#70) — nothing is missing, and the CoQ still cannot be issued

Its foreign matter *is* covered by CNP, and it **failed**: `Страни материи 0.08 %` against a
maximum of `2.00 %`, marked `(Не одговара)`, with the conclusion *НЕ ОДГОВАРА … поради
утврдено присуство на семе од канабис*.

Its iCoA needs Ident C only, like its eleven siblings. But the CoQ cannot be compiled as
conforming, and no in-house work changes that. **This row is a blocker, not a gap** — it needs
a disposition, not an iCoA.

### 2 · Seven batches cannot be classified as initial or reissue

The gap analysis flags 21 batches for a reissued CoQ because they carry a 197-series analysis.
**Fourteen also carry earlier testing**, so the 197 really is a re-analysis and a reissue
follows. The other seven carry **nothing but** the 197 pair — no potency, no microbiology, no
contaminants, no earlier certificate of any kind:

`P060152` · `P060212` · `P060242` · `P060332` · `P060352` · `P060382` · `P060402`

Calling that a *reissue* asserts a first CoQ with nothing behind it. Their names are P-numbers
rather than cultivation batch codes, and their strains (Jokerz 31, Jelly Donutz, Orange Punch
Mimosa, Cash Cow, Fat Bastard, Scrambler, Gorilla Glue) all appear on earlier bulk batches —
which reads like **packaged lots drawn from bulk already released**. If that is right, the 197
analysis is their initial release testing and their CoQ should reference the bulk batch's
certificates.

The register does not say which. They are marked `197-only` in the CSV, anchored on the 197
date so they still fall in sequence, and the question is left where it belongs.

### 3 · `GG1024` (#81) is not in the register

One certificate on file, `in_register=N`, no date. It needs a full-panel iCoA and has no
chronological anchor. It sits last in the register and is marked `not in register`.

### 4 · An iCoA does not unblock a CoQ on its own

**38 of the 80 register batches have no microbiology recorded and 37 have no physico-chemical
testing.** For those, the iCoA can be issued but the CoQ still cannot be compiled until the
outsourced panel arrives. The CSV carries an `outsourced_outstanding` column so the two
constraints are visible separately — 37 of the 38 are 2026 batches, consistent with testing in
flight rather than testing skipped.
