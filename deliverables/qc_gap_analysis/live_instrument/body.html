01 The three registersТрите регистри
How the CoQ universe is built

One initial Certificate of Quality for every batch on record, first to last, and a 12-month cannabinoid + mycotoxin reissue for every batch. Sixty-one carry numbers from the owner's ISSUE_COQ plan — 48 initial for Tranche 01 (19) and Tranche 02 (29), and 13 additional-testing CoQs, CoQ-PP-2026-0027 … 0039, for the lots whose retest fell due. The rest are predicted: their number is copied from the issuance record at issue, never computed in advance. A CoQ is never labelled "retest" — each is an ordinary sequential certificate of quality for the batch at its date of issue.

No CoQ prints an issue date before , when the CoQ SOP was put in use. The plan's 2025 and 2026 dates are packaging dates — the basis of the numbering series — not issue dates. Every document below therefore carries two: its basis date, and the earliest date it may be issued on — the SOP date, the newest document it cites, or (for a reissue that cites nothing yet) its 12-month due date, whichever is latest. The window is two-sided: no iCoA or CoQ could have been issued before 11.05.2026, and none is ever post-dated — a document is dated the day it is signed. Every printed date therefore lies between the SOP date and today, and the desks below refuse anything outside that window. The only certificates that legitimately carry 2025 dates are the old QCCoA 001 v.01/v.02 in-house CoAs — the pre-SOP documents, with their own codes and issue dates; each is superseded by the corresponding CoQ, doc code and issue date assigned only from 11.05.2026 onward, in chronological order. And issuance may proceed in batches: when the parameters missing from the outsourced certificates finally arrive, every CoQ they were holding up is issued together — one date, sequential numbers, chronological order — at the batch-issuance desk on the CoQ tab.

On a reissue only ten determinations are re-certified — identity A, B and C and the assay from Farmahem together, foreign matter on an in-house iCoA, and the three mycotoxins. Every other determination reads outside the retest scope — the release determination stands on the initial CoQ.

02 Microbiological purity — every criterion, checkedМикробиолошка чистотаPh. Eur. 5.1.8 category C

All microbiological purity testing is Ph. Eur. 5.1.8 category C. Seventeen IPH certificates name the category on the parameter line itself — Microbiological quality (Ph.Eur. 5.1.8 cat. C) — Total aerobic microbial count (TAMC) ≤ 10^5 CFU/g — and Purely Plant's in-house CoA form writes Ph. Eur. 5.1.8, category C. Against that, all seven criteria are correct as the specification states them.

№ParameterПараметар Category CThis registerVerdict Results over it

Ph. Eur. 5.1.4 reads an enumeration criterion of 10ⁿ CFU/g as a maximum acceptable count of 2 × 10ⁿ — "10¹ CFU: maximum acceptable count = 20; 10² CFU: 200; 10³ CFU: 2000, and so forth" — so ≤ 10⁵ conforms up to 200 000 and ≤ 10⁴ up to 20 000. It governs enumeration criteria only, never an absence criterion. No TAMC result in this register is over any reading of its criterion: the highest is 5.1 × 10⁴, a quarter of the maximum acceptable count. Every microbiological exceedance is TYMC.

Four findings recorded rather than changed, each a QA determination on a signed document: Purely Plant's in-house CoA form multiplies by five, printing <10^5, max 500 000 and <10^4, max 50 000 where the pharmacopoeia gives 200 000 and 20 000 — three documents carry it and no disposition turns on it, but the form issues on every batch it is used for; the HPA1024 in-house CoA prints <10^ CFU/g for bile-tolerant GNB, with no exponent; six certificates apply criteria tighter than category C to a category C product, conservatively and with every result conforming anyway; and QCSP 001 cites the categories as 2.6.12 cat. C, 2.6.31 cat. C, 2.6.13 cat. C — those are the test methods, the categories live in 5.1.8, which is how the certificates cite them.
