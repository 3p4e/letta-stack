# The microbiological retest ruling — 17.09.2026

> **The Head of QC, 17.09.2026**
>
> "If the certificate for microbiological purity from the external laboratory is at a newer
> date, meaning we have retested that parameter, you will also have to adjust the dating,
> the date of issue of the certificate of quality accordingly."

Two instructions in one sentence. The first — a newer external microbiology certificate is a
**retest** of #9 — the desk half-believed already. The second — **the date of issue moves with
the document** — is new, and it is the rule the desk was missing.

---

## 1 · What the desk was doing, and why it was wrong

`testing_series` has said since 10.09.2026 that the IJZ-MB delivery of 25/26.08.2026 is one
campaign sampling and that every certificate in it is a retest document. The intake of
16.09.2026 wrote twenty-nine of those certificates into the register for exactly that reason.

But the carry of 15.09.2026 only ever fills an **empty** row: a retest certificate takes the
initial result for a determination the retest did not run. So the campaign's microbiology
reached a certificate only where the initial round had none:

* **ten lots** had no initial microbiology at all — their reissue took the campaign result and
  has been printing it since;
* **nineteen lots** did have initial microbiology — and their reissue went on printing 2025
  counts, with the campaign certificate for the same lot sitting unprinted in the register.

The ruling settles which of the two a reissue prints. **The campaign's.** It was a retest, and
a retest certificate states the retest.

The second half is the v35 date rule read forwards. v35 says a certificate may cite only a
document issued on or before its own day; the desk had been applying it as a veto — refusing
the newer document and leaving the older one printed. The Head of QC says the other resolution
is the right one: **take the document and move the certificate.**

**One certificate was re-dated.** `CoQ-PP_26-089` (P050212) was issued **17.08.2026** and its
new microbiology is dated **31.08.2026** — the page as it stood could not have cited it. The
standing issuance rule moves it: `issuance_schedule.coq_issue`, five to ten days after the last
external certificate the page cites, held at `LAG_DAYS = 7`. **17.08.2026 → 07.09.2026**, and
`last_external` with it, from 10.08.2026 to 31.08.2026. `coq_register_2026-09-10.csv` was
restamped so the register and the certificate state the same day.

**Twenty did not move, and that is the rule working rather than an exception to it.** A reissue
already dated after its new microbiology could have cited it all along; nothing about it needs
adjusting. The nineteen Tranche 3 reissues of 21.09.2026 postdate their 31.08/01.09.2026
microbiology by three weeks, and `CoQ-PP_26-110` of 18.09.2026 postdates it by eighteen days.

A date the owner set is not re-derived, either. The Tranche 3 reissues are dated five days
after their own campaign's potency certificate of 16.09.2026 — inside the owner's "five to ten
days", which is the range `LAG_DAYS = 7` stands in for. Only a document the certificate could
not have cited moves a date.

## 4 · The eighty-nine release certificates were not touched

Twenty-nine release certificates have a lot whose newest microbiology postdates them. Not one
changed, and the reason is the ruling of 10.09.2026, not a gap in this one: the campaign
certificate is a **retest document**, so no release certificate may rest on it whatever the
dates say. A release certificate states the round that released the lot.

Every one of the twenty-nine has a reissue, and every one of those reissues now carries the
campaign result. Nothing on file goes unprinted.

## 6 · Every other determination, checked the same way

The ruling was given for microbiological purity. The desk swept every other determination group
for the same situation — a reissue citing something older than the newest document on file that
determined the whole group:

| group | reissues citing something older |
| --- | ---: |
| potency (#4–#6) | 0 |
| **loss on drying (#8)** | **7** |
| microbiology (#9.1–#9.5) | 0 — after this pass |
| mycotoxins (#10) | 0 |
| heavy metals (#11) | 0 |
| pesticides (#12) | 0 |

Five of the seven are not really seven: P050282, P050292, P050302, P050312 and P050322 differ
only because the register files the CNP certificate under the in-house CoA's number and date,
so the "newer document" is the same document under a second name. That is `OI-51`.

**Two are genuine.** `CoQ-PP_26-095` (P050022) prints ППК25139 of 22.05.2025 where ППК25174 of
10.07.2025 is on file; `CoQ-PP_26-153` (J31112501) prints 051-5-ГС/26 of 02.03.2026 where
100-1-ГС/26 of 09.04.2026 is. Both newer documents **predate** their certificate, so under this
rule the citation would move and the date would not.

Nothing was changed there. The Head of QC ruled on the microbiological-purity certificate and
the desk applied the ruling to microbiological purity. `OI-52` puts the extension to the Head of
QC — for loss on drying, and for whether the rule should stand over **every** determination
rather than be asked family by family.

Loss on drying did get its own ruling the same day, but a different one: which **laboratory** a
certificate takes the figure from, not which date. That is in
`LOSS_ON_DRYING_RULING_2026-09-17.md`, and it leaves the date question above exactly where it
stands.
